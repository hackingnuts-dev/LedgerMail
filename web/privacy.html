Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 24, 2026

In brief: LedgerMail is a personal financial-email organizer. It uses the Gmail read-only permission you choose to grant to identify receipts, payments, bills and financial reminders. It does not send, delete or modify your Gmail messages. It does not sell your Google data or use it for advertising.

Who operates this app

LedgerMail is an independently developed application. For privacy questions or requests, contact alankarsharma44@gmail.com.

Google account information we access

After you authorize access through Google's consent screen, LedgerMail uses your verified email address to identify your connected mailbox and the Gmail read-only scope (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.readonly) to retrieve Gmail messages, including sender, subject, date, labels, snippet and email body where needed for financial classification. Some non-financial messages may be retrieved while filtering and are not intended to appear as financial transactions. Google sign-in uses identity information needed to confirm the account.

How your information is used

LedgerMail analyzes messages to show user-facing transaction summaries, receipts, bills, amounts, dates and review states. Results are estimates derived from emails, not authoritative bank or payment records. Your Gmail data is not used for targeted advertising, sold to data brokers or used to assess creditworthiness or eligibility for lending.

Where information is stored

Current browser-based app: account-specific financial summaries, review states and some message metadata are saved locally in your browser's storage. The browser-based Google access token is kept in memory and may require another authorization after expiration.

If the server-side background-sync feature is enabled: an explicitly authorized Google OAuth refresh credential, account identifier and a bounded cache of Gmail messages (up to 500 raw messages per account in the current implementation) are stored in the application's Netlify backend, encrypted at rest using an application-managed key. The backend accesses Gmail to refresh the cache while your phone is locked; the app imports those results when opened. A signed, secure, HttpOnly browser session cookie is used for returning visits. Scheduled synchronization is periodic, not guaranteed instantaneous. Netlify may process operational metadata and server logs as the hosting provider.

These features may not yet be enabled on every deployment; check the application's connection and sync status for the active behavior.

Sharing and Google API Services User Data Policy

Google data is shared with service providers only to provide the functions you authorized, such as Google providing Gmail API access and Netlify hosting background-sync infrastructure when enabled. LedgerMail does not share Google user data with advertisers, data brokers or other third parties for their independent marketing purposes. Human access to Gmail content is not a feature of LedgerMail and would occur only with your affirmative consent for specific content, when necessary for security, or when required by law.

LedgerMail's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Retention, disconnecting and deletion

Local saved financial history remains on your device until you clear it in the app or clear that site's browser data. If server-side sync is enabled, choosing Disconnect Gmail removes the connected account's server-side OAuth credential and associated server message cache. Disconnecting does not automatically erase local history, which has a separate deletion control. Cached server messages have a size limit but no guaranteed automatic time-based deletion in the current implementation. Contact the address above if you need assistance deleting data.

Security

LedgerMail uses HTTPS for communications. The proposed background-sync backend encrypts stored mailbox data and uses secure session cookies. No system can guarantee absolute security. Do not share Google OAuth client secrets, encryption keys or session credentials in chat, screenshots or source code.

Your choices

You may decline Gmail authorization, disconnect the account in LedgerMail, clear stored history, or revoke LedgerMail access through Google Account connections. Revoking Google access prevents new Gmail access but does not by itself guarantee deletion of cached local or server data; use LedgerMail's deletion controls as well.

Changes to this policy

We will update this page when our handling of Gmail data changes. Changes that materially expand uses of Google data require an updated disclosure and, where applicable, your renewed consent before that use begins.
